# Step 2: His room (2 images)

The character needs somewhere to sit. A real room makes the video feel like a creator filming at home. You make the room once, then a second sheet shows it from other angles so the model keeps the room the same.

- **Model:** Nano Banana Pro
- **Time:** 5 minutes for both images

## Image A: the room

- **Aspect ratio:** 4:5 (or 9:16 if you will post vertical only)
- **Save as:** `room.png`

Pick the 6 things first, in the same spirit as the character:

| # | Decide | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Window view** | A place that feels like you. Blue hour looks best | Hillside harbor city at dusk |
| 2 | **Desk setup** | Matches your work | Wooden desk, laptop with code, microphone arm, brass lamp |
| 3 | **Wall feature** | Tied to your topic or your story | Corkboard with red string (investigating), a wall of old keys |
| 4 | **Bed or sofa** | Gives depth behind the character | Low bed with indigo linen |
| 5 | **Floor and rug** | A pattern adds richness | Terracotta tiles, woven kilim rug |
| 6 | **Light** | One warm lamp plus one cool window | Amber desk lamp, blue moonlight |

```
Photorealistic interior photo of [ROOM TYPE, e.g. a self-taught builder's bedroom-workshop] at blue hour turning to night. Shot from the doorway at standing eye level with a wide 24mm lens. The room is empty, with no people.

LAYOUT: Back wall center: [WINDOW + VIEW]. Left wall: [DESK SETUP]. A [CHAIR] stands at the desk. Above the desk: [WALL FEATURE]. Right side: [BED OR SOFA]. Above it: [SHELVES AND OBJECTS]. Floor: [FLOOR AND RUG]. Walls: [WALL COLORS]. A [CEILING LIGHT] hangs from the ceiling.

LIGHTING: Warm and cold at once. The warm amber light comes from [WARM SOURCES]. The cool blue light comes in through the window. Soft haze, gentle falloff, calm and quiet. [WHAT TO AVOID: e.g. no neon, no RGB LEDs].

Photorealistic, cinematic architectural interior photography, film grain, 8k, sharp focus, no text, no logos, no flags.
```

**Tip:** the room must hold a **chair you can turn to face the camera**. Chair, desk and wall feature are what the viewer sees behind the character in the finished video.

## Image B: the room sheet

- **Upload:** `room.png` as `@image_1`
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9
- **Save as:** `room-sheet.png`

This is the most important one for consistency. Its "TALKING ANGLE" panel is the exact camera view of your video.

```
Using @image_1 as the exact reference, create a multi-angle environment reference sheet of THIS SAME room. Keep every piece of furniture, object, color, wall art, rug pattern and lighting identical and in the same positions. Do not add or remove objects.

LAYOUT: A light warm-grey sheet background with clean uppercase labels under each panel. Left: one large panel showing the original main view, labeled "MAIN VIEW". Right: a 2x3 grid of smaller panels:
- "REVERSE": the opposite wall, seen from the window, showing the doorway and the foot of the bed
- "WINDOW": the window with the view, with the edge of the desk
- "BED": the bed and the shelves above it
- "DESK": a close-up of the desk setup, with the wall feature above it
- "TALKING ANGLE": the camera at the window at chest height, looking back toward the chair, which is turned to face the camera, with the desk and the wall feature softly out of focus behind it
- "TOP VIEW": an overhead floor-plan view of the entire room

Photorealistic, the same warm lamplight and cool window light in every panel, 8k.
```

## Check the result

- [ ] Image B shows the **same** room as image A (same rug, same shelves, same colors)
- [ ] The "TALKING ANGLE" panel has a clean chair and a nice, not-too-busy background
- [ ] No people and no readable logos
